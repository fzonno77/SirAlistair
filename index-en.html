THE PROLOGUE - Sir Alistair’s Secret: A Story Outside the Lines

Every proper fairy tale begins with a knight without fear or flaw, ready to fight fire-breathing dragons to protect the kingdom. And for a long time, Sir Alistair’s story was exactly like that. Page after page, chapter after chapter, always the same routine: polish the armor, mount the horse, face the monster, save everyone, start all over again.

But behind that shining helmet, Sir Alistair was hiding a secret. He was officially fed up.

He was tired of the weight of that armor creaking with every step, of princesses who screamed far too much and, let’s be honest, he had had enough of fighting. Sir Alistair no longer wanted to be the perfect hero of a story that had already been written. He had a secret dream, a dream scented with powdered sugar, cinnamon and melted chocolate: he wanted to become a pastry chef!

So, one night, while the author of the book was fast asleep and the readers had closed the page, Sir Alistair made an incredible decision. He laid down his sword, removed his iron gloves and decided to do the one thing a character of his importance was never supposed to do...

He decided to escape from his own book in search of happiness.

But how do you escape from a story? And what happens if, on the road to freedom, our pastry-chef knight encounters a dragon who, instead of breathing fire, is hiding a secret as big as his own?

THE STORY - A white hole in the page... Where did the hero go?

It all begins in the quiet library on Cherry Street. Little Leo is reading his favorite book, "Sir Alistair and the Valley of Dragons", when something never seen before happens: the words begin to tremble and slide off the paper. Only a white silhouette remains at the center of the page. Sir Alistair is gone!

Who brought Sir Alistair to life?

Hi, I’m Francesco! Sir Alistair’s story has a special origin: it began with stories I wrote when I was little, when I was a boy dreaming of adventures at school. Some time ago, I happened to find them again in an old drawer at my parents’ house. Reading them again made me realize that the same magic could speak to children today.

I chose to preserve the spirit of those stories, turning them into a book written by a young person for young readers. I firmly believe that nobody is better placed than someone who has just lived through the age of discovery to understand, move and engage their peers. Sir Alistair is not just a story; it is my way of showing that the voice of young people, when it is authentic, has the power to break down every barrier — even the one between the real world and the world of books.
